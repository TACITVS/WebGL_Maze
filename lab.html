Dungeon invariants

How the layout is made

Each floor is a tile map. A BSP partition chops it into uneven leaves, a room of random size and shape is carved into most of them, and a few leaves stay solid rock so the floor has negative space. A minimum spanning tree over the room centres becomes corridors, plus extra links so the dungeon has loops rather than being one branching tree.

The third dimension

Floors are stitched together by switchback stair towers, dropped wherever a room on one floor overlaps a room on the next. A tower punches a stairwell through the upper floor, stands as a walled tower on the lower one, and is entered only through its mouth. Placement is rejected unless both floors stay fully connected without routing through the tower.

Making it a dungeon

Rooms are typed from their size and their place in the graph — entrance, vaults on dead ends, crypts, cisterns, a boss chamber at the deepest point — and furnished accordingly. Locks are placed only on bridges of the room graph, and each key is hidden in the region still reachable without it, so every dungeon is solvable by construction.

Validation

The checks above do not trust the generator. They flood-fill the compiled world with the same collision query the player's own movement uses, so a pass means a person can really walk from the entrance to the goal — not merely that a hand-drawn centreline happens to be supported.
